## NEWSLETTER BRIEF: The Hidden Cost of Protocol Amendments

This piece explores the operational and timeline impact of mid-trial protocol amendments, based on an internal analysis of Power's site network data across 12 neuropsychiatry trials over the past 18 months, combined with observations from recent sponsor conversations.

The angle is that protocol amendments are often treated as minor administrative updates, but our data shows they create cascading delays that extend far beyond the amendment approval timeline itself. What makes this interesting is the counterintuitive finding that sites with the fastest enrollment rates actually experience the largest disruption from amendments - their momentum is harder to restart. The audience should walk away understanding that amendment planning needs to account for "restart costs," not just regulatory approval time.

---

### The main threads to pull:

**First, the hidden timeline impact.** Our data shows that the average protocol amendment adds 6-8 weeks to trial timelines, but the formal amendment approval process only accounts for 2-3 weeks of that. The rest is "restart cost" - retraining coordinators, re-consenting enrolled patients, and rebuilding recruitment momentum.

**Second, high-performing sites are hit hardest.** Sites in the top quartile for enrollment saw 40% longer restart periods than bottom-quartile sites. The hypothesis: their recruitment pipelines are fuller, so more patients need re-screening or re-consent, and coordinator attention is diverted from active recruitment.

**Third, amendment timing matters more than amendment scope.** Amendments during active enrollment cause 3x more disruption than those during startup or close-out. This suggests sponsors should consider "amendment windows" in protocol design.

**Fourth, what sites wish sponsors knew.** From our coordinator feedback: the biggest pain point isn't the amendment itself, it's the lack of advance warning and the assumption that sites can absorb the change without impact to other studies.

---

### Evidence to anchor the piece:

- Average timeline extension: 6-8 weeks total vs. 2-3 weeks for approval alone
- Top-quartile sites: 40% longer restart vs. bottom-quartile
- Enrollment-phase amendments: 3x disruption vs. startup/close-out amendments
- 73% of coordinators cited "lack of advance notice" as top frustration (internal survey, n=48)
- Example: [Anonymized Trial X] saw enrollment drop 60% in the month following a mid-enrollment amendment, took 11 weeks to return to prior rate

---

### Context the writer should know:

Protocol amendments are a fact of life in CNS trials - FDA feedback, emerging safety signals, and competitive dynamics all drive changes. The industry conversation focuses on regulatory strategy (how to get amendments approved efficiently), but largely ignores the operational reality at sites. Our readers live this frustration but may not have data to quantify it or advocate for change with sponsors.

---

### Worth acknowledging:

- This is Power network data, which may skew toward certain site types (largely academic and community neurology)
- We can't isolate the "amendment" variable perfectly - some trials had other disruptions
- Sponsors face real constraints that drive amendments; we're not saying amendments are avoidable, just underestimated in impact
- Sample size is 12 trials; patterns are suggestive, not definitive
